Two facts from your code raise the stress-case cost:

- 
Shield and hull are kit sync fields (ShipCombatComponent.shield is a SyncFieldFloat; hull is the vehicle's currentHp), and fire and impacts are AllRpc. ### The ship-sender interface (Part 2), for both sides to agree

- 
Your payload stays yours. ShipMovementComponent keeps its message and your Phase 0 header [mode][flightMode][epoch], and gains one method, WriteState(sampleTimestamp, writer, out sendReliably). The sender calls it at most once per ship per send tick and copies the bytes unchanged to each recipient.

- 
The ship stays registered as a kit movement handler. Owner uploads (BaseGameNetworkManager.cs:584) and client reads (:599) both look it up. While the sender owns the ship, the kit-facing WriteServerState returns false and the kit skips it (:703-704).

- 
No client change. The sender emits ordinary EntityState (message 119) packets in the kit's record layout. The one condition is that ReadServerStateAtClient consumes exactly the bytes it was given, synchronously; yours does today.

- 
The bytes describe the ship at sampleTimestamp. Extrapolate AI from the last physics step and mirrors from the owner's upload time. Otherwise observers trail by 5–12.5 m E.

- 
Discrete events travel as state (teleport sequence, mode, epoch). A DiscreteVersion counter makes a ship urgent: it goes to every subscriber on three builds in a row. No reliable channel is needed.

- 
Frame changes: withhold in both directions whenever the observer's applied frame is not known to be the ship's frame.

- 
Who owns what. You own IShipSendPolicy (locks, attackers, wing, seats, supercruise, frame-change state) and the per-client byte budget. The add-on owns the mechanism: budget enforcement, fairness, repeats, withholding, packet assembly, timing.

- 
What remains in the kit loop. It still visits every (client, ship) pair each tick: about 6 ms on each tick frame in the stress case E. Only a kit patch to the loop at BaseGameNetworkManager.cs:695-704 removes it; scope it after the G1 measurement.

- 
The grid send path must stay disabled. It is, by default, in your kit copy (DefaultGridManagerComponent.isDisabled = true). That path never calls a handler's WriteServerState, so your ship message would not go out at all.

- 
To agree: the byte budget; whether to add a compact payload variant for the stress case; whether flightMode counts as a discrete event; packing mode and flightMode into one byte (saves a third of the 3 B header). The C# contract sketch is in Part 2, §8.

### BubblePolicy (Part 3)

- 
Thresholds as functions of your AOI. With τ = t + L_max = 2 s:
| Quantity | Value |

|---|---|

| D_merge, armed pair | 3.7–4.0 km (set by the 3 km lock range, not the AOI) |

| D_merge, unarmed pair | 3.3–3.6 km |

| D_split | 14 km (by capability, not current speed) |

| Drop-join radius J | 14 km |

| Roam keep R_out / entry R_in | 29.5 km / 22 km |

| Neighbourhood gap for placement D_nbr | 69 km |

| Never offload across a gap below | 34 km |

| Flapping | at most one merge-and-split cycle per 50 s per pair |

- 
Mechanism, in your order: a merge, split or roam exit is a batch of per-group frame changes. The server moves every group of the batch in the plan frame; each client applies and acks; the batch's visibility flips once, on the last ack or at a deadline. Flipping per group would make the ships of one fight despawn and respawn for each other.

- 
One metric for the AOI and the policy. The kit AOI is X/Z only. We recommend 3D for both: a copy of the kit AOI class as your own BaseInterestManager with the Y axis on (no kit patch), 3 km cells, and bounds that stay inside the kit's int cell index. If the AOI stays X/Z, BubblePolicy must use X/Z too.

- 
Deliverable: inputs and outputs, pseudo-code, determinism rules, and 60+ table-driven test cases (failure cases first). We will review P1's BubblePolicy against that table.

### Findings for your plan, most important first

- 
Missile lock (3 km) is longer than the largest normal-space visible range (2.625 km). Decide whether a lock needs visibility. For armed ships the lock range, not the AOI, sets D_merge.

- 
Flip visibility per batch, not per group (above).

- 
Gameplay physics queries must filter by SubChannelId. A moved group shares slot geometry with its target bubble until its ack, and physics does not know sub-channels. ShipAIPilot and ShipCombatComponent use physics queries.

- 
ShipEntity has no GetInfo override, so a ship as instigator of kit damage reports an empty sub-channel, and that damage is refused once targets carry bubble ids (DamageableEntity.cs:416). Your ship weapons use TakeShipDamage, so this only matters for kit damage paths.

- 
Seated characters should carry their ship's visible range. At the default 80 m, a remote client sees a ship whose pilot spawns only within 80 m.

- 
Low cruise inside a bubble: with R = 30 km the policy's invariants fail above about 525 m/s. Faster low cruise must be a handoff moment (your "promotion" moment covers it if it is).

- 
Kit client read path: after a successful handler read the kit does not move to the end of the record, and an async void reader escapes its catch. Recommended vendor patch: reader.SetPosition(recordStart + length) after BaseGameNetworkManager.cs:609.

- 
Teleport patch scope. The clears are at BuiltInEntityMovementFunctions3D.cs:1375-1378: jump (1375), dash (1376), teleport (1377), still-move (1378); your :1376 is the dash clear. IsJump and IsDash have the same first-observer-only bug. The grid path repeats it in CreateMovementData (:1329-1332), and NavMeshEntityMovement repeats the teleport clear (:595-596, :629-630). We have no patch. We would like to carry yours in our kit copy too, so both copies stay the same.

- 
Two more kit patches to scope after G1: no movement write for a seated passenger unless a flag is pending; and a send loop that visits only the entities the kit still sends.

### Summary

- 
Frame rate does not change the send cost. The kit writes every subscribed entity to every client on every 20 Hz tick. That is per-second work, so going from 60 to 30 fps leaves its share of the CPU the same. What changes is timing:

- 
the tick frame is now 1.5× the frame average (it was 3×);

- 
a tick falls on 2 of every 3 frames.

- 
The kit does write seated pilots, once per subscriber per tick. A pilot is subscribed only by its owner and by players within the pilot's own 80 m range. That is 1–4 writes per pilot per tick E: 0.2–0.8 % of movement writes in (b), 0.8–3.1 % in (a), and at most 1.3 % of bytes. Pilots matter in other ways:

- 
each one is a second kit entity per player, with a per-frame cost;

- 
their recovery sync fields feed the sync scan, which loops over every player on the process for every dirty element;

- 
their writes carry no information, because clients snap passengers to the seat every frame.

- 
Case (a): 64 players + 64 AI, all in view.

- 
Kit path CPU: 1.25–2.71 ms/frame on average and 1.7–3.8 ms on the tick frame. That is at most 11 % of 33.3 ms, so CPU is not the problem.

- 
The downlink is the problem: 172–173 kB/s per client (1.38 Mbit/s), above the proposal's G1 limit of 100 kB/s, and 11 MB/s per process.

- 
Ship sender: 0.55–0.97 ms/frame, or 0.43–0.57 with two kit patches. Downlink is 118 kB/s at their 60 B payload, which is still above 100 kB/s, or 60 kB/s quantized.

- 
Case (b): 400 players + 100 AI, all in view.

- 
Kit path: 955–2,014 ms of main-thread send work per second. That is 31.8–67.1 ms/frame on average and 43.5–93.7 ms on the tick frame. With the rest of the frame, demand is 1,650–3,230 ms of main thread per second. It cannot run at any per-pair cost in the 0.1–0.3 µs range.

- 
Kit egress: 668 kB/s per client, 267 MB/s (2.14 Gbit/s) per process, 272k datagrams/s.

- 
Ship sender: 12.7–20.4 ms/frame, or 10.0–11.9 ms with the two kit patches. Downlink is 411 kB/s per client at 60 B, which is 164 MB/s (1.31 Gbit/s) per process and more than one 1 GbE link. Quantized, it is 208 kB/s per client.

- 
Under their AOI, every visible ship is within about 2.6 km: 2,000 m + (250 + 250 m/s) × 1.25 s = 2,625 m at boost. None reaches the proposal's 1–2 Hz tier (beyond 10 km), and most sit in its 20 Hz tier (≤ 2 km). The far-tier savings the proposal counted on never happen.

- 
In (b), the sender cuts send CPU (movement plus sync and events) by 2.5–3.3× at equal per-pair cost, or 3.2–5.6× with the patches. The proposal's M1 figure was about 2.7 ms/frame at 60 Hz replacing 14–30 ms, i.e. 5–11×. Design A's 13.5× does not hold either.

- 
It cuts bytes by only 1.5–1.6× at their payload.

- 
The proposal's 52–65 kB/s per client needs a quantized payload, plus a byte budget that drops ships beyond gun range to about 2 Hz.

- 
Whole frame for (b), rate-corrected: 33.1–52.5 ms (99–158 %) with the patched sender. The proposal's "~24 ms (71 %)" does not hold.

- 
One-process cap at the 80 % line, with everyone mutually visible: about 170–310 players + 100 AI E.

- 
Agree two points before either side edits ShipMovementComponent (§6 point 7):

- 
The ship handler must stay registered, because the server's inbound path and the client's read path both look it up.

- 
The client read path trusts each handler to consume exactly the bytes it wrote.

### 1. How the kit sends movement C

Rates in this repo vs theirs

Rate | This repo | Theirs |

Our setup window flags only frame rates above 60, and then offers "Set 60" (Assets/ServerMeshing/Mesh/Editor/ServerMeshSetupWindow.cs:382-392).

Tick scheduling

- 
LogicUpdater(1/updateFps) is built at LNL/Scripts/LiteNetLibManager.cs:155 and advanced every frame (:159-162) by an accumulator loop (LNL/Scripts/Utils/LogicUpdater.cs:88-110).

- 
At 33.3 ms frames and 50 ms ticks the pattern is 0, 1, 1 ticks per frame, so 2 of 3 frames send.

- 
A long frame makes the next frame run 2 ticks back to back when it lasts at least 66.7, 83.3 or 100 ms. Which one applies depends on where the accumulator stood: at 30 fps it holds 33.3, 16.7 or 0 ms between frames. A frame of 100 ms or more always causes a double tick.

- 
The loop allows up to 5 ticks per frame, then drops the backlog (LogicUpdater.cs:7,99-102). Catch-up ticks resend unchanged positions.

- 
Physics at 50 Hz gives 1, 2, 2 steps per frame. Two of every three frames have 2 physics steps and two of every three have a send, so at least one frame in three has both.

What runs on each tick (LNL/Scripts/GameApi/LiteNetLibGameManager.cs:109-121, BGNM:129-137)

- 
The sync-field pass.

- 
The AOI countdown, on a 1 s interval (Kit/Core/Scripts/Networking/AOI/JobifiedGridSpatialPartitioningAOI.cs:18,175-178). It completes in LateUpdate of the same frame (:248-337).

- 
The movement send.

The send loop (BGNM:661-766)

The grid path is off by default (Kit/Core/Scripts/Networking/GridManager/DefaultGridManagerComponent.cs:16-23), so BGNM:133-134 takes the SendServerEntityMovementState path. For each player the loop does four things:

- 
Reset two writers (:683-694).

- 
For every subscribed id (:695-697): a ConcurrentDictionary lookup (:699, type at :108), a writer reset (:701), then WriteServerState (:703).

- 
Copy the result in, with a packed object id and a 4-byte int length (:708-710, :734-736).

- 
Flush when the next entry would reach 1,020 B, i.e. position + entry + 8 ≥ 1,020 (:717-733; the limit is at LNL/Scripts/GameApi/LiteNetLibGameManager.StateSyncing.cs:12).

Every subscribed entity is written every tick, with no change check.

- 
The kit handler returns true whenever _isStarted is set (BIEMF:1340-1341,1379).

- 
The interface has no receiver parameter (Kit/Core/Scripts/Gameplay/Interfaces/IEntityMovementDataHandler.cs:17), so a handler cannot tier or skip per client.

- 
A player's own objects are always in its subscriptions (LNL/Scripts/GameApi/BaseInterestManager.cs:65-66), so V includes the player's own ship.

- 
Whether their ShipMovementComponent returns false when nothing changed depends on their code. It could only do that per entity, not per receiver.

Packets

- 
The header is 12 B:

- 
message type 119 as a packed value: 1 B (Kit/Core/Scripts/Networking/GameNetworkingConsts.cs:25, LNL/Scripts/Extensions/NetDataWriterExtension.cs:181-184);

- 
the packed long timestamp: 7 B. It is written as two zigzag packed ints (NetDataWriterExtension.cs:161-165); the high word (about 416) takes 2 B and the low word 5 B;

- 
a 4 B count.

- 
A 57 B ship entry costs 64 B with its framing: a 3 B packed id plus a 4 B length.

- 
A Ids between 2,288 and 67,823 take 3 B (NetDataWriterExtension.cs:192-197), and ids only grow (LNL/Scripts/GameApi/LiteNetLibIdentity.cs:668-682).

- 
15 entries fit in a packet: 12 + 15 × 64 = 972 B. A 16th would need 972 + 57 + 8 = 1,037 ≥ 1,020.

- 
On the wire, add 1 B (LiteNetLib) + 28 B (UDP/IPv4). That is 1,001 B per 15 entries, i.e. 66.7 B per ship entry. The proposal assumed 61 B.

- 
The transport sends with peer.Send (LNL/Scripts/Transports/LiteNetLib/LiteNetLibTransport.cs:120). That call:

- 
goes to SendInternal, which copies the packet into a pooled buffer and queues it under a lock (LNLib/LiteNetPeer.cs:605-684; the queue is at :679-684);

- 
is drained by LiteNetLib's single logic thread every 15 ms (LNLib/LiteNetManager.cs:113,498-537; LNLib/LiteNetPeer.cs:1302-1319).

- 
The MTU stays at 1,024 B. MTU discovery is off by default and nothing in this repo turns it on (LNLib/LiteNetManager.cs:244; LNLib/LiteNetPeer.cs:317; LNLib/NetConstants.cs:89-103).

- 
A 973 B packet is below the direct-send threshold of 1,001 B (LiteNetPeer.cs:1187-1200), so it goes into the merge buffer (:1202-1209).

- 
Two such packets never fit in 1,024 B, so each still leaves as its own datagram (SendMerged, :1161-1184), after one extra copy.

- 
So the main thread pays a handoff per packet, and one network thread pays for every sendto.

The client read path (BGNM:589-617)

- 
Each packet carries one timestamp and a count. For each entry the client reads a packed id and an int32 length, looks up the handler, and calls ReadServerStateAtClient.

- 
It skips by the length only when no handler is registered or the read throws (:599-604, :610-615).

- 
After a successful read it does not seek to the end of the entry. The handler must consume exactly the bytes it wrote.

- 
A short read silently shifts every later entry in that packet. Their Phase 0 header must be written and read in lockstep.

- 
The server side needs the handler as well. An inbound client state is dropped unless the object has a registered handler owned by the sending connection (BGNM:579-587).

Which component their pilot prefab uses depends on their code.

- 
So the kit writes a seated pilot once per subscriber per tick.

Who subscribes? The visible range belongs to the observed object.

- 
The AOI queries a sphere around each target using the target's own range (JobifiedGridSpatialPartitioningAOI.cs:219; BaseInterestManager.cs:56-59,67).

- 
The grid ignores Y (JobifiedGridSpatialPartitioningAOI.cs:122, disableYAxis = true).

- 
A pilot is therefore seen by its owner plus the players within 80 m in X/Z. Their "characters see 80 m" is the character's own visible range. That gives 1–4 writes per pilot per tick (E; it depends on formation spacing).

- 
The driver owns the ship (Kit/Core/Scripts/Gameplay/BaseGameEntity_MountFunctions.cs:105-107; LNL/Scripts/GameApi/LiteNetLibAssets.cs:594-596). The client sends the ship's movement, and passengers send nothing (BGNM:620-627, 639-660).

Sync fields and RPCs of seated characters

- 
A sync field goes only to the identity's subscribers (LNL/Scripts/GameApi/LiteNetLibSyncElement.cs:9-12).

- 
But the pass loops over every ready player on the process × every dirty element, every tick, whoever subscribes (StateSyncing.cs:378-392).

- 
That is O(players) per dirty element, and O(P²) overall once the number of dirty elements grows with players.

-

### 2. Inputs and rate classes

Rate classes, and how each converts to a 33.3 ms frame:

- 
[T] per tick: the cost per second is fixed. Average per frame = per-second ÷ 30, and the whole tick lands on one frame.

- 
[F] per frame: the cost per frame is unchanged.

- 
[P] per physics step: 1.67 steps per frame.

- 
[E] per event: the cost per second is fixed.

Converting the proposal's per-entity numbers. e, x_P and x_AI were per 60 Hz frame.

-

calls WriteServerState and skips the record on false (703-704).

- 
A record is PutPackedUInt(objectId), Put(int32 length), then the payload (708-710 reliable, 734-736 unreliable).

- 
Unreliable packets split at MAX_UNRELIABLE_PACKET_SIZE = 1020 (SS:12; BGNM:715-733), with an 8 B record-header estimate (717).

- 
Delivery is ReliableOrdered (747) or Unreliable (725, 758), both on MOVEMENT_DATA_CHANNEL = 2 (BaseGameEntity.cs:17).

- 
Consequence: WriteServerState runs once per (client, entity) pair per tick. That repeated serialization is what the sender removes. It is also the root of the teleport bug: the flags are cleared inside each call (BIEMF3D:1375-1378).

Grid/job path (BGNM:767-945)

- 
It calls CreateMovementData for every subscribed handler (826-834) and writes the kit's quantized, yaw-only format itself (864; DGMC:105-127).

- 
No handler can opt out.

- 
In this repo it is off by default (DGMC:16-23).

Client read

- 
HandleServerEntityStateAtClient (BGNM:589-618) is registered for msg 119 at FH:51.

- 
It reads the timestamp (592) and the int32 count (593). Then, per record, the packed objectId (596) and the int32 length (597).

- 
If no handler is found, it skips the record by length (599-605) and logs a warning (601).

- 
Otherwise it calls ReadServerStateAtClient(peerTimestamp, reader) (609); a throw is caught and the record skipped (611-616).

- 
After a successful read it does not re-seek. A handler that reads more or less than length corrupts the next header, and that header read is outside the try.

- 
The catch cannot see exceptions from an async void handler. C# posts those to the SynchronizationContext and the call returns normally. The kit's own reader is async void (BIEMF3D:1395).

Uploads from owner-simulated player ships

- 
The client sends the driven vehicle's WriteClientState (BGNM:620-637; the sync entity is the vehicle, 639-660).

- 
The server looks the handler up in the same dictionary and requires handler.ConnectionId == sender (BGNM:579-587). The upload's peerTimestamp is passed through (582, 586).

Subscriptions and spawns

- 
Subscribe calls identity.AddSubscriber, which raises onSubscriberAdded (LNP:40-47; LNI:691-699), and appends a spawn (LNP:46).

- 
The default AOI applies subscriptions in its LateUpdate every updateInterval = 1 s (JGAOI:18, 175-178, 248-333).

- 
The spawn goes out ReliableOrdered on the object's sync channel at the next tick's game-state sync (LNGM:111; SS:400, 451). It carries raw position and Euler angles only (SS:243-248).

- 
Kit movement records for an object sent before that are skipped by the client (BGNM:599-605).

### 2. Item (1): payload, header and WriteState [their code]

- 
Payload. It stays theirs: [mode u8][flightMode u8][epoch u8] + today's ~57 B. Each mode has a fixed length, so the reader always consumes exactly length.

- 
The WriteState contract:

- 
Append only. Other ships' bytes precede and follow in the shared buffer. Never Reset or SetPosition. The sender truncates back to the start if it returns false.

- 
No side effects. A second call in the same build writes identical bytes.

- 
Discrete events are state, never flags cleared on write. A cleared flag would reproduce the kit bug at BIEMF3D:1375-1378.

- 
Describe the ship at sampleTimestamp.

- 
AI and parked rigidbodies were last stepped up to 20 ms earlier (50 Hz physics), so extrapolate by velocity to sampleTimestamp.

- 
A player-ship mirror holds the owner's last upload, stamped with the client's peerTimestamp (BGNM:582, 586). Extrapolate by sampleTimestamp − uploadTimestamp.

- 
Without this, observers trail by speed × staleness: 250 m/s × 20 ms = 5 m for AI; 125 m/s × (50 ms one-way + about 50 ms wait) ≈ 12.5 m for mirrors E.

- 
Return false to send nothing on this build, as the kit does before _isStarted (BIEMF3D:1340-1341).

- 
DiscreteVersion increments when the teleport sequence, mode or epoch takes a new value. Continuous motion never changes it.

- 
Include flightMode only if it changes rarely. Every increment costs 3 builds × all subscribers.

- 
Example E: 500 ships each switching flightMode once per 10 s → 50 × 399 × 3 ≈ 59,900 records/s. That is ≈ 150/s per client, or 16 % of the 940/s that 64 kB/s allows.

- 
IsAtRest is true when a velocity-extrapolating observer would see no change.

- 
Unknown modes cannot be skipped by the handler, which never sees length (IEMDH:15). Keep client and server builds in lockstep.

- 
Recommended Phase-0 kit hardening (record it in VENDOR_PATCHES): after BGNM:609, always run reader.SetPosition(positionBeforeRead + dataLength);. This makes every handler, including the kit's async void one, unable to corrupt the next record. The sender does not require it.

### 4. Item (3): fan-out in exactly the kit's record format

Packet layout (one per client, per build, per delivery method):

[packed ushort 119][packed long sampleTimestamp][int32 count] { [packed uint objectId][int32 length][length bytes] } × count

Built only with public APIs:

- 
TransportHandler.WritePacket (TransportHandler.cs:90-96)

- 
PutPackedLong / PutPackedUInt (NDWE:161-215)

- 
Put(int) and Put(byte[], off, len) as at BGNM:709-710

- 
ServerSendMessage(conn, MOVEMENT_DATA_CHANNEL, method, writer) (LiteNetLibManager.cs:362-365)

Rules

- 
The sender uses its own writers, not the kit's internal statics (EntityMovementDataBuffers.cs:7-10).

- 
Exact record size = packed objectId + 4 + length.

- 
The objectId packs to 1 B up to 240, 2 B up to 2,287, 3 B up to 67,823, 4 B up to 16,777,215, and 5 B above (NDWE:181-215).

- 
Ids are never reused (LNI:668-683), so a long-running process reaches 3-byte ids.

- 
The sender flushes before a packet would pass 1,020 B.

- 
The largest payload that fits is 1,020 − 12 − 9 = 999 B. A larger one is skipped and logged once.

- 
A packet never mixes builds.

- 
The host connection is skipped (BGNM:676-677).

- 
The sender's packets are separate messages from the kit's character packets (BGNM:589-618).

Client change: none. Three conditions on their side [their code]:

- 
The ship's handler sits in the client dictionary under its objectId, which is the kit pattern (BIEMF3D:222-226).

- 
ReadServerStateAtClient consumes exactly length bytes, synchronously, before any await.

- 
It is not async void, or it catches internally. Otherwise the kit's catch never runs and the rest of the packet is misparsed (§1).

Recipients. The sender walks identity.GetSubscribers() (LNI:726-729) and never adds recipients, so AOI and SubChannelId isolation are inherited unchanged (LNI:803-831; BaseInterestManager.cs:61-67). Subscription events (LNI:72-73, 695, 705) drive per-pair state.

Per build

- 
At most once per Unity frame, so catch-up ticks send nothing twice.

- 
Hook LogicUpdater.OnTick in OnStartServer and unhook in OnStopServer.

- 
The manager subscribes first (LiteNetLibManager.cs:221-226), so the sender runs after the kit. Unhooking keeps that order across a stop and restart.

- 
Read ts = ServerTimestamp once.

- 
For each ship whose DiscreteVersion changed:

- 
mark it urgent for its current subscribers;

- 
re-classify all its pairs in this build, before anything is written.

- 
Ship-major pass. For each (ship, subscriber) pair:

- 
classify if due or dirty;

- 
skip it if Withheld;

- 
add credit (rate × elapsed);

- 
the pair is due if credit ≥ 1, an urgent repeat is pending, or a first-send is pending.

- 
If any pair of the ship is due, call WriteState once and add (slice, priority, staleness, urgent) to each due client's candidate list.

- 
Client-major pass. Urgent and reliable candidates first, then routine ones by priority and then staleness, up to the budget. Deferred candidates keep their credit.

- 
Spread each build's sends over this frame and the next. The header keeps the build timestamp. Pending records for a pair unsubscribed in between are dropped.

### 6. Item (5): reliable and unreliable

LiteNetLib's Unreliable delivery can drop, duplicate or reorder packets (NetConstants.cs:9-11).

Record | Delivery | Why | 
Routine sample | Unreliable | The next sample supersedes a lost one. | 
Teleport sequence change, mode 0↔1 | Unreliable, urgent: every subscriber on the change build + 2 repeats | ReliableOrdered on channel 2 is shared with the kit's reliable records (BGNM:741-748); one loss delays all later reliable ones. Chance all 3 are lost (independent loss): 1e-6 at 1 %, 1.25e-4 at 5 % E. A loss burst of ~100 ms defeats it, but every later sample still carries the state. | 
Frame change (epoch+1, mode 2) to the moving group's clients | Unreliable, every build until the ack | Repeating until the ack gives reliable delivery without head-of-line blocking. | 
Withhold (both directions) | No records at all, not even urgent or first-send | Applies whenever the observer's applied frame is not known to be the ship's frame (see below). | 
FrameChange control data, if separate | Their own ReliableOrdered message | – | 
New subscriber | Nothing on a build in the tick of the subscription event; first state on the next 3 builds | The spawn is written only at the next tick's game-state sync (LNGM:111; SS:400, 451). It carries no velocity, epoch, sequence or mode (SS:243-248), and its ordering against unreliable movement is not guaranteed. | 
sendReliably = true | ReliableOrdered EntityState packet, as at BGNM:706-712, 740-748 | Escape hatch. Counts against the budget and is never deferred. | 

- 
Withhold covers both directions:

- 
the moving group's ships, to observers outside the group;

- 
ships outside the moving group, to the group's clients, from the move until those pairs are unsubscribed.

- 
When visibility flips. The flip happens on the next AOI pass, up to 1 s later (JGAOI:18, 175-178), unless their code calls UpdateInterestManagementImmediate (JGAOI:163-167).

- 
Urgent records may exceed one build's budget by up to 1×. The rest moves to the next build, ahead of routine records.

- 
The kit teleport bug cannot happen on the sender path: WriteState runs once per build and clears nothing. Their Phase-0 kit patch is still needed for characters.

### 7. Item (6): timestamps and the epoch

Timestamps

- 
ServerTimestamp is Unix milliseconds on the server (LNGM:66-74; RttCalculator.cs:6).

- 
The client estimates server time as local time plus a ping-synced offset (RttCalculator.cs:7, 26-30).

- 
One sample timestamp per build goes in every packet header of that build, and the same value is passed to WriteState. The bytes describe the ship at that time (§2).

- 
Spreading and deferral never restamp. Restamping would add up to 250 m/s × 33 ms = 8.3 m of error.

Epoch

- 
It is per ship, inside their header. The sender ignores its value and sees changes only through DiscreteVersion.

- 
It is 1 byte, compared wrap-safely: (sbyte)(incoming - applied) > 0 means newer.

Client rules [their code]:

- 
Drop samples with an older epoch.

- 
Hold newer-epoch samples until the frame is applied.

- 
Drop duplicate or older samples by timestamp.

- 
Treat the first sample after a spawn as the baseline.

- 
Reset the last-accepted timestamp on an epoch change, on moving to another process, and on a backwards clock step.

- 
Another process may run on a host whose clock differs by its NTP offset, and the client re-derives its offset after reconnecting.

- 
Without the reset, the equivalent check (BIEMF3D:1417) silently drops samples.

The per-client packet-header epoch (proposal lines 264, 592) is withdrawn. The client reads the timestamp and then the count (BGNM:592-593), so there is no spare byte without a client change.

// Add-on: Assets/ServerMeshing/Mesh/ShipSync/ShipSyncContracts.cs (proposed; not written)
using LiteNetLib.Utils;
using LiteNetLibManager;

namespace MultiplayerARPG.MMO.Mesh.ShipSync
{
 /// <summary>
 /// IShipStateSource is the game's side of the ship-sender contract, implemented by ShipMovementComponent.
 /// The game owns every byte WriteState writes and how the client reads it; the sender decides only
 /// when WriteState runs and which subscribers receive an unchanged copy of its bytes.
 /// </summary>
 public interface IShipStateSource
 {
 /// <summary>ObjectId is the ship's network object id and the record key in EntityState packets.</summary>
 uint ObjectId { get; }

 /// <summary>Identity is the ship's network identity. The sender reads its subscribers and ConnectionId (the owner); it never changes them.</summary>
 LiteNetLibIdentity Identity { get; }

 /// <summary>
 /// DiscreteVersion changes whenever a field changes that every observer must see promptly: the teleport
 /// sequence, the mode or the epoch (the flight mode only if it changes rarely). It never changes for motion.
 /// </summary>
 uint DiscreteVersion { get; }

 /// <summary>IsAtRest is true when a velocity-extrapolating observer would see no change (parked, docked, stopped).</summary>
 bool IsAtRest { get; }

 /// <summary>
 /// WriteState appends this ship's complete payload, header first ([mode][flightMode][epoch]), describing the
 /// ship as of sampleTimestamp (extrapolated from the last physics step or the last owner upload).
 /// It is called at most once per ship per build. It must only append (never Reset or SetPosition),
 /// must not change state (a second call writes identical bytes), and must carry discrete events as state.
 /// It returns false to send nothing on this build.
 /// </summary>
 /// <param name="sampleTimestamp">Server timestamp (ms) of this build; every packet carrying these bytes has it in its header.</param>
 /// <param name="writer">Shared per-build buffer; other ships' bytes precede and follow these.</param>
 /// <param name="sendReliably">True routes the record to the recipient's ReliableOrdered EntityState packet.</param>
 bool WriteState(long sampleTimestamp, NetDataWriter writer, out bool sendReliably);
 }

 /// <summary>IShipStateRegistry is the sender's registration surface on the map network manager's GameObject.</summary>
 public interface IShipStateRegistry
 {
 /// <summary>Register hands the ship's server-to-client state to the sender; false means it stays on the kit path (no sender, or grid path enabled).</summary>
 bool Register(IShipStateSource ship);

 /// <summary>Unregister forgets the ship on despawn or handoff.</summary>
 void Unregister(IShipStateSource ship);

 /// <summary>MarkRelevanceChanged re-classifies one pair on the next build (lock, attack, wing, seat).</summary>
 void MarkRelevanceChanged(uint shipObjectId, long observerConnectionId);

 /// <summary>MarkShipChanged re-classifies every pair of one ship on the next build (its group starts or ends a frame change).</summary>
 void MarkShipChanged(uint shipObjectId);

 /// <summary>MarkObserverChanged re-classifies every pair of one observer on the next build (its group starts a frame change, or it acknowledged one).</summary>
 void MarkObserverChanged(long observerConnectionId);
 }

 /// <summary>ShipObserverContext describes one (ship, observer) pair when the sender asks the policy for a rate.</summary>
 public readonly struct ShipObserverContext
 {
 public ShipObserverContext(long observerConnectionId, float distance, bool isOwner)
 {
 ObserverConnectionId = observerConnectionId;
 Distance = distance;
 IsOwner = isOwner;
 }

 /// <summary>ObserverConnectionId is the receiving client.</summary>
 public long ObserverConnectionId { get; }

 /// <summary>Distance is the 3D distance from the observer's player character (seated: at the seat) to the ship.</summary>
 public float Distance { get; }

 /// <summary>IsOwner is true when the observer simulates this ship.</summary>
 public bool IsOwner { get; }
 }

 /// <summary>ShipSendRate is one observer's routine rate for one ship and its rank when that observer's budget is short.</summary>
 public readonly struct ShipSendRate
 {
 /// <summary>
 /// Withhold sends nothing, not even urgent or first-send records. Return it whenever the observer's applied
 /// frame is not known to be the ship's frame: the observer's group is mid frame change and the ship is not in
 /// that group, or the ship and the observer are in different frames and still subscribed.
 /// </summary>
 public static readonly ShipSendRate Withhold = new ShipSendRate(0, 0, true);

 public ShipSendRate(byte hz, byte priority) : this(hz, priority, false) { }

 private ShipSendRate(byte hz, byte priority, bool isWithheld)
 {
 Hz = hz;
 Priority = priority;
 IsWithheld = isWithheld;
 }

 /// <summary>Hz is 0, 1, 2, 5, 10 or 20 (clamped to the send rate); 0 still receives urgent and first-send records.</summary>
 public byte Hz { get; }

 /// <summary>Priority orders routine records when the budget is short; higher goes first.</summary>
 public byte Priority { get; }

 /// <summary>IsWithheld is true only for Withhold.</summary>
 public bool IsWithheld { get; }
 }

 /// <summary>IShipSendPolicy decides routine rates. The game implements it; the first one found on the network manager's GameObject wins, else DistanceShipSendPolicy.</summary>
 public interface IShipSendPolicy
 {
 /// <summary>Classify returns the rate for one pair; called at the classify rate and after any Mark call or DiscreteVersion change. Must not allocate.</summary>
 ShipSendRate Classify(IShipStateSource ship, in ShipObserverContext observer);
 }
}

PolicyResult Evaluate(PolicyInput input, PolicyState st)
{
 if (Validate(input, st) is string err) return Rejected(err, st); // tests F1-F9
 var s = Scene.Build(input); // effective bubble, frozen flag, f_b per effective bubble
 var edges = new List<Edge>();
 foreach (var (p, q) in s.CandidatePairs(cell: DSplitMax)) // 27 neighbours (9 in XZ)
 {
 if (p.Group == q.Group) continue;
 double d = Dist(p, q); // config metric (§4)
 if (p.Host != q.Host) { if (d <= DMerge(p, q)) Report(CrossHostConflict, p, q, d); continue; }
 bool same = s.Eff(p) == s.Eff(q);
 if (d <= (same ? DSplit(p, q) : DMerge(p, q))) edges.Add(Edge.Ship(p, q, d));
 if (!same && d <= E(p, q) && !s.Vetoed(p, q)) Report(PopInRisk, p, q, d); // must stay 0
 }
 foreach (var b in s.LocalPersistent) // body links: capture R_in,b / keep R_out,b
 foreach (var g in s.GroupsNear(b.Bodies, ROut(b)))
 if (MinDist(g, b.Bodies) <= (s.Eff(g) == b.Id ? ROut(b) : RIn(b))) edges.Add(Edge.Body(g, b));
 foreach (var l in input.Engagements) // unknown member, other host, or > EngagementMax
 if (Valid(l)) edges.Add(Edge.Engagement(l)); else Report(EngagementIgnored, l);

 var uf = new ConstrainedUnionFind(s, st.Vetoes); // cannot-link: a set holding a SideA group never
 foreach (var e in edges.OrderBy(Kind != Engagement).ThenBy(Len).ThenBy(IdPair)) // joins one holding a SideB group
 uf.TryUnion(e); // engagement across a veto releases it (report)

 var plan = new PlanBuilder(input.Now, LMax);
 foreach (var c in uf.Components().OrderBy(c => c.MinGroupId)) Resolve(c, plan, st);
 var vetoes = ReleaseVetoes(st.Vetoes.Concat(plan.NewVetoes), s); // §2 veto rules below
 return new PolicyResult(null, plan.Build(),
 new PolicyState(plan.DetachedSince, vetoes, plan.NextVetoId, input.Now));
}

void Resolve(Component c, PlanBuilder plan, PolicyState st)
{
 var touched = c.EffectiveBubbles; // sorted
 var keeperOf = touched.Where(b => Keeper(b) == c).ToList(); // Keeper: body node, else most clients, groups, min id
 bool beyond = c.Groups.Any(g => DistToAnchor(g, Eff(g)) > ROut(Eff(g)));
 if (touched.Count == 1 && keeperOf.Count == 1 && !beyond) return; // settled
 if (touched.Count == 1 && keeperOf.Count == 0 && !beyond) // detached part: split timer
 {
 foreach (var g in c.Groups) plan.DetachedSince[g] = st.DetachedSince.GetValueOrDefault(g, plan.Now);
 if (plan.Now - c.Groups.Max(g => plan.DetachedSince[g]) < T_SPLIT) return;
 if (c.Groups.Any(Frozen)) { plan.Report(Deferred, c); return; } // timer kept
 plan.Move(c.Groups, plan.NewBubbleAt(AabbCentre(c)), MoveReason.Split); return;
 }
 var home = ChooseHome(c, keeperOf); // merge (touched >= 2) or roam exit
 switch (home.Kind)
 {
 case HomeKind.Existing: case HomeKind.New:
 plan.Move(c.Groups.Where(g => Eff(g) != home.Id), home, touched.Count >= 2 ? Merge : RoamExit); break;
 case HomeKind.Defer: plan.Report(Deferred, c); break; // a frozen group would have to move
 case HomeKind.PersistentEdge: CutAway(c, IneligibleGroups(c, home), PersistentEdgeCut); break;
 case HomeKind.None: CutLongestMstEdge(c, OverflowCut); break; // recursion: Resolve both halves
 }
}

Home ChooseHome(Component c, List<Bubble> keeperOf)
{
 if (c.BodyBubble is Bubble b) // >1 body: cut on the body-body path first
 return Eligible(c, b) ? Home.Existing(b) : Home.PersistentEdge(b); // persistent absorbs only with its body (team)
 var best = keeperOf.Where(x => x.Kind == BubbleKind.Dynamic && Eligible(c, x))
 .OrderBy(x => MoveCost(c, x)).ThenBy(x => x.CreatedAt).ThenBy(x => x.Id).FirstOrDefault();
 if (best != null && MoveCost(c, best) < INF) return Home.Existing(best);
 if (c.Groups.Any(Frozen)) return Home.Defer;
 var (centre, rho) = AabbCentre(c); // order-independent
 return rho <= R_IN ? Home.New(centre) : Home.None;
}
bool Eligible(Component c, Bubble x) => c.Groups.All(g => Eff(g) == x.Id
 ? DistToAnchor(g, x) <= ROut(x) // staying tolerates R_out
 : !Frozen(g) && DistToAnchor(g, x) <= (x.Kind == BubbleKind.Persistent ? ROut(x) : R_IN)); // entering a dynamic bubble needs R_in
double MoveCost(Component c, Bubble x) => c.Groups.Where(g => Eff(g) != x.Id).Sum(g => Frozen(g) ? INF : 10 * g.Clients + 1);

Plan rules:

- 
Each group appears in at most one batch per call. Each component produces at most one batch, plus recursion after a cut.

- 
In-transit groups count as members of their target bubble, and they are never re-planned.

- 
Merge homes are limited to bubbles the component keeps, meaning it holds their keeper. A merge of two detached parts gets a new bubble at once, instead of moving into one bubble and splitting out 30 s later.

- 
Cuts remove the longest MST edge that is not an engagement link (ties broken by id pair).

- 
Each cut adds a veto (SideA, SideB) and reports VetoCreated. If the cut edge is ≤ E, it also reports VetoedWithinE.

- 
If no cuttable edge is left, report Overflow and leave the component as it is. Members may then overrun R, which is reported as RoamOverrun.

When a veto is released (checked each call, in id order):

- 
either side has no present group;

- 
the minimum distance between the sides is > D_split,max;

- 
an engagement link spans the two sides;

- 
the union of both sides would now have a feasible home (ChooseHome returns Existing or New).

Without the last condition, a veto between two fleets chasing each other would never end.

What the executor must guarantee. It follows the team's order: the server moves each group, each client applies the new frame and acks, and only then does visibility flip. The thresholds in §3 assume all of the following.

- 
Move: in the plan frame, for every group of the batch.

- 
Set Rigidbody.position (never MovePosition), then Physics.SyncTransforms.

- 
Move seated characters explicitly. The LateUpdate seat snap (K7) would be one frame late for hit checks.

- 
Shift hit-box histories (K9) and the batch's kit damage entities by Δ.

- 
Send each client its frame change.

- 
Leave SubChannelId unchanged.

- 
Drop every subscription between the batch and old-bubble entities outside it.

- 
Normally there are none: every old-bubble member within D_split of a mover is in the same component, hence in the batch. Only a cut leaves one.

- 
Without the drop, such an entity would receive the mover's new-slot positions until the next pass.

- 
Flip: once per batch.

- 
When every client of the batch has acked, or at Deadline, whichever comes first: set SubChannelId = target on every identity of the batch in one server frame, and call GetInfo() on each (K8).

- 
Groups with 0 clients need no ack. A batch of such groups only flips in the plan frame.

- 
Stragglers. A group whose ack misses the deadline flips alone when its ack arrives. It is reported.

- 
Until then it sits in the target slot, hidden by sub-channel.

- 
Gameplay physics queries must filter by SubChannelId.

- 
Resync it after 6 s (E, N1).

- 
It never flips before its ack: kit spawns carry raw floats (K11).

- 
Re-subscription of the batch against its new bubble.

- 
The next regular AOI pass is enough. §3 shows why the pass interval does not enter τ.

- 
For a late flip (a straggler, or a Deferred merge), a targeted re-subscription of the batch at the flip shortens the gap (N2).

- 
Never call UpdateInterestManagementImmediate in a frame where the regular pass is already scheduled:

- 
it clears and refills the grid (AOI.cs:182-203) while the pending jobs run until LateUpdate (AOI.cs:248-256);

- 
it overwrites that pass's per-object result lists (AOI.cs:217-218).

- 
At most one pass per frame, counting the regular one. Each costs ≤ 2 ms p99 (team).

- 
Spawns go out at the next 20 Hz tick either way (K11).

- 
Decoding during the transit window. The window runs from the move to the client's apply.

- 
Ship states and uploads carry the epoch (team's Phase 0 header), so both directions decode each sample in its own frame.

- 
Server to client: the frame change goes out in the plan frame, on the reliable-ordered channel that carries spawns (K11). Later reliable messages on that channel are then decoded after it. Unreliable kit character states are not ordered with it, which is harmless only for seated characters (K7).

- 
Client to server: other messages that carry positions (kit attack or skill) have no epoch. The server decodes them in the old frame until that client's ack and in the new frame after it. This is exact only if they travel on the ack's reliable-ordered channel. Otherwise they need an epoch field. This depends on their weapon code.

- 
In-transit reporting.

- 
The executor reports InTransitTo and InTransitSince from the move until the flip.

- 
It refuses a NewBubble when no slot is free. The policy then re-plans at the next check.

- 
It may give a new bubble the slot of a bubble that the same batch empties. Moving to another slot is still a frame change, never an anchor move.

Why merges show no pop-in.

- 
Say the pair crosses D_merge at time t0.

- 
The check sees it by t0 + t, and the server moves the movers in that frame. The batch's visibility flips by t0 + t + L_max = t0 + τ.

- 
The distance closed over τ is ≤ Δ(τ). So at the flip, d ≥ D_merge − Δ = E_pq, which is ≥ max(A_p, A_q) at reach speeds and ≥ both interaction ranges.

- 
From the flip on, the pair is in one bubble at d ≥ A. That is the state of any pair born in one bubble.

- 
The next regular pass (≤ T_aoi later) subscribes it at d ≥ A − (v + f)·T_aoi = A0 + (v + f)·(T_lead − T_aoi).

- 
The spawn is then shown within T_spawn.

- 
So the kit's lead needs T_lead ≥ T_aoi + T_spawn: 1.25 ≥ 1 + 0.25, with 0.018 s to spare against the 0.232 s estimate E.

- 
This is why the pass interval (or a targeted re-subscription) is not added to τ.

- 
If the team changes T_aoi, T_lead must change with it.

The guarantee fails only for a straggler past L_max, or for a Deferred merge (N2). Both are counted.

### 4. The merge predicate must match the AOI predicate: X/Z or 3D

What the AOI predicate is today (K1–K3): a pass subscribes observer o to target x iff d_XZ(o, x) ≤ A_x. Because of sub-channel isolation (K5), the predicate matters only inside a bubble. So the policy's job is to put p and q in one bubble before they would satisfy the AOI predicate if they already shared one.

That requires the same four things on both sides:

- 
the same metric;

- 
the same range owner (the pair max, K1);

- 
the same positions;

- 
the same range function (AoiRange.Of).

Positions match automatically. sim = slot + (U − anchor) is a pure translation, so inside a bubble universe distances equal sim distances within the float step. That step is ≤ 3.1 cm within 500 km of the process origin (proposal §2.1).

Must BubblePolicy use X/Z? Yes, while the AOI stays X/Z: consistency needs one metric. But X/Z is wrong for a 3D space game.

Recommendation: switch both to 3D.

- 
One config switch, DistanceMetric, shared by the AOI and the policy.

- 
Roam (R, R_out, R_in), overflow radii (ρ) and the slot/physics checks are always 3D, whatever the switch. Float precision and physics are 3D.

What breaks if they differ. Example: two ships 1 km apart in X/Z and 20 km apart vertically.

- 
Policy 3D, AOI X/Z:

- 
While they are in one bubble, the AOI subscribes them (d_XZ = 1 km ≤ A). Bytes are sent every tick, and each client shows a contact 20 km away.

- 
Once d_3D > D_split (14 km) for 30 s, the policy splits them. The contact vanishes from a client that was showing it: a visible pop-out.

- 
The same pair in two different bubbles is never merged. So whether a client sees that contact depends on bubble history, not on geometry.

- 
Across bubbles, nothing pops in: isolation hides everything.

- 
Policy X/Z, AOI 3D:

- 
It never under-merges, since d_XZ ≤ d_3D.

- 
But it merges vertically separated groups into "columns". Their 3D radius then overruns R_in, which forces overflow cuts across edges the X/Z metric calls short. VetoedWithinE fires for pairs that are really 20 km apart.

- 
Bubbles grow, and so do fan-out and batch sizes.

- 
Both X/Z: consistent, but every bubble is a column.

- 
A fleet stacked vertically is one bubble, sent in full.

- 
Roam (3D) and linking (X/Z) disagree, so overflow cuts appear.

- 
Both 3D: consistent and correct.

Getting a 3D AOI without a kit patch. Add their own SpaceGridAOI : BaseInterestManager, a copy of the kit class with disableYAxis: false.

- 
The kit uses any BaseInterestManager on the network manager's GameObject (BGNM.cs:120-122).

- 
It has to be a copy, not a subclass: PrepareSystem is public but not virtual (AOI.cs:102), and _system is private (:22).

- 
Cell size in 3D ≈ A, about 3 km. A query spans 2A = 5.25 km:

- 
3 km cells: 2–3 cells per axis, 8–27 probes per query E;

- 
1 km cells: 6–7 cells per axis, 216–343 probes E;

- 
1 km cells in X/Z, for comparison: 36–49 probes E.

- 
The flat cell index is an int, x + GX·(y + GY·z) (SPS/QueryFunctions.cs:53). So GX·GY·GZ must stay below 2³¹ ≈ 2.1·10⁹:

- 
1 km cells over a 1,000 km cube give 10⁹, close to the limit;

- 
3 km cells give 334³ ≈ 3.7·10⁷.

- 
AOIMapBounds must cover every slot ± R in all three axes (AOI.cs:110-118).

- 
Objects outside the bounds are clamped into edge cells (SPS/UpdateGridJob.cs:30).

- 
A query whose extent lies wholly outside the bounds on an axis scans that whole axis: an out-of-range minimum cell resets to 0, an out-of-range maximum to the last cell (SPS/QuerySphereJob.cs:40-52).

- 
The 3D switch also removes the kit's own mismatch: 3D at spawn, X/Z per pass (K3).

- 
No AlwaysVisible on bubble entities (K5).

- 
Seated characters should carry their ship's A. At the default 80 m range, remote clients would see a ship whose pilot spawns only within 80 m (BaseInterestManager.cs:10).

### 5. The roam boundary with fixed anchors

Radii (3D, per bubble b with roam R_b):

- 
R_out,b = R_b − 2·V_g,max·t = 30,000 − 250·2 = 29,500 m.

- 
The first t covers the interval between checks.

- 
The second t is one check of slack for a roam exit that is Deferred or whose NewBubble is refused.

- 
The ack round trip does not count, because the server moves the group at the plan.

- 
A group past R_out,b at a check is therefore moved before it reaches R_b.

- 
R_in,b = R_out,b − V_g,max·T_roam = 29,500 − 7,500 = 22,000 m. This is the entry limit into a dynamic bubble.

- 
For a persistent bubble, R_in,b is the body capture radius, and R_out,b is the body keep radius and its entry limit.

- 
Planets: R_b = r + h (team), so R_out,b = R_b − 0.5 km and R_in,b = R_b − 8 km.

- 
EngagementMax = 2·R_in = 44 km. A longer engagement link is ignored and reported (EngagementIgnored).

- 
Config invariants (checked by Create):

- 
R_in > 0;

- 
2·R_in ≥ D_split, so any same-bubble pair fits in one bubble (44 ≥ 14 km);

- 
D_split > D_merge,max.

Rule. Roam exit is per component, never per group. A group linked to others can only leave with them; otherwise the linked groups would lose sight of each other.

- 
If any group of a component is beyond R_out of its effective bubble, ChooseHome runs with that bubble ineligible for the groups that would stay.

- 
The component then goes to one of:

- 
an eligible bubble it keeps. This is the "neighbouring bubble" case, and it is exactly the merge case: an unlinked neighbour would only split it off again after 30 s;

- 
otherwise, a new bubble anchored at its AABB centre, if ρ ≤ R_in;

- 
otherwise, it is cut.

- 
The whole component moves in one batch: it moves in one frame and flips in one frame.

Hysteresis, and why it cannot flap:

- 
Staying tolerates R_out. Entering a dynamic bubble needs R_in.

- 
After a roam exit, every member is within ρ ≤ R_in of the new anchor. The next roam exit needs a member to cover ≥ H = 7.5 km, which takes ≥ 30 s at boost.

- 
Returning to the old bubble needs a link to its keeper and a position within R_in of its anchor: again ≥ 7.5 km back inside, ≥ 30 s.

- 
Bound: ≤ 1 roam move per group per 30 s.

- 
A lone ship flying straight changes frame every 29,500/250 = 118 s at boost (236 s at cruise). That is ≤ 60/118 = 0.51 frame changes per client per minute E. Nothing is re-subscribed, because it is alone.

Persistent bubbles (the body and the anchor never move):

- 
A persistent bubble is home only if its body node is in the component (team).

- 
A fight straddling R_out,b cannot move out: the body cannot move, and the edge members cannot enter a new bubble without losing the fight.

- 
The policy cuts the longest non-engagement MST edges on the paths from the body to the ineligible groups.

- 
It reports PersistentEdgeCut, plus VetoedWithinE if a cut edge is ≤ E.

- 
This is the one designed seam. Size R_b so that nothing is fought near it.

- 
A component with two bodies (a chain of ships between stations) is cut on the body-to-body path first.

Hard limit:

- 
A group beyond R_b is reported as RoamOverrun. That is a group that cannot move (RoamBlocked), or a roam exit deferred or refused more than once.

- 
Ack stragglers never overrun: the server moved them at the plan.

- 
The physics margin gives ≤ 14 s (E, §3) before a physics query could reach the next slot. After that, the executor forces the move or the handoff and counts it.

- 
A hot host holding a single bubble may raise R_b, since Roam is a per-bubble input. At 100 km the float step is 7.8 mm (proposal §2.1). This makes whole-fight roam exits (N7) rarer.

### What depends on the team's code (confirm before building)

- 
Group composition: which entities belong to a ship group (seated characters, missiles, drones, towed objects), and the Clients count per group.

- 
ShipEntity GetInfo (K8), and whether ship weapons use kit damage entities (the instigator refresh at the flip).

- 
Physics queries: whether lock and AI detection are physics queries, which sets the physics-margin budget (14 s vs 18 s). Whether gameplay physics queries filter by SubChannelId, which stragglers require (N1).

- 
Whether a lock needs visibility. Lock is 3 km; A is at most 2.625 km.

- 
The measured L per client (apply + ack, p50/p95/p99/p99.9 under load), which sets L_max at the p99.9 or above.

- 
Targeted re-subscription at late flips (stragglers, Deferred), or the regular pass only.

- 
Unseated kit characters: whether any exist in space bubbles (Movable = false).

- 
Station and planet R_b values.

- 
The send path: the grid manager stays disabled, or their ship sender replaces both kit paths. Otherwise the ship message and its epoch header are not sent (K10), and the teleport patch must cover both paths (K6).

- 
Low cruise: whether it is flown inside a bubble, and its top speed. Above about 524 m/s the invariants fail at R = 30 km (§3).

- 
Position-bearing client messages (attacks, skills, fire requests): which channel they use, and whether they need an epoch field (§2).
