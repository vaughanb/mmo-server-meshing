> **From the large-systems plan to the server-meshing design (2026-09-25; revision 6.1, 2026-09-26).** This is what the large-systems work guarantees and what it needs from you. It matches the contract you were given (bubble = partition unit; a planet is owned by one process; supercruise = kinematic doubles seen by 3D distance, not partitioned geographically; supercruise entry/drop = the handoff seam; persistence = (frame id, local)). **Revision 6 (2026-09-25) folded in your review:** isolation by `SubChannelId`, a shared `BubblePolicy`, the admission queue at the drop, your handoff timing, and the ship-sender interface. **Revision 6.1 (2026-09-26) is the reply we agreed after your answer of 2026-09-25:** no anchor moves (your BubblePolicy spec, Part 3, is what our P1 builds), a 3D AOI, the ship-sender contract of your Part 2 §8, ships' base visible range 3 km, planet roam, low cruise, group composition, position-bearing RPCs, and your findings (items 1, 3, 5, 9-14 updated; item 15 new).

> 1. **The bubble is the partition unit and is never split across processes.** Each bubble has a process-independent name `BubbleKey = Body(bodyId) | Space(uuid)`, and every networked identity in it carries the sub-channel `b:<bubbleKey>` (`LiteNetLibIdentity.SubChannelId`; group-private `g:<rootObjectId>` in supercruise and during single-group frame changes, while a batch keeps its channels until its flip; `limbo:<id>` when stowed). The kit then refuses visibility, damage and pickup across bubbles, and it fails closed: an untagged identity is hidden from every tagged player. Runtime ids (`BubbleId = process epoch + counter`, never reused) and sim slots are process-local and never persisted or sent to other services. **No bubble's anchor ever moves**; a new anchor appears only as a new bubble (item 11).
> 2. **Planet ownership.** A landable planet has exactly one bubble (`Body(bodyId)`), which never splits or merges, and one server landscape instance; it must live on one process. (A fallback exists for planets that fail precision in a secondary slot: several *surface patch* bubbles of one planet — these must also stay on the planet's process, because the height cache and LOD arbiter are per process.)
> 3. **Per-process layout.** `SlotLayout.Build(hostedBodies)` runs over the bodies a process hosts. Hosting constraint per process: ≤ 5 landable planets and every secondary planet within the per-axis precision budget (largest planet at the sim origin, the others on ±X and ±Z at R_b0 + R_bi + 10 km); a 150 km planet must be the largest on its process. Space slots sit on two Y planes at a 70 km pitch, capped at the slots within 15.6 mm per axis (98 in the examples). Limbo is a lattice of 2 km cells at y = −1,000 km. A slot is never released while any entity is registered in its bubble; parked ships of dormant space bubbles are stowed in limbo. You may host fewer bodies per process freely.
> 4. **Exclusive membership.** Every networked space entity is in exactly one of: a bubble; the per-system `SupercruiseRegistry` (U in doubles, velocity, rotation); limbo-pending (arrival or frame change in progress, group-private channel; or the dead crew of a ship lost in a dynamic space bubble, awaiting respawn in a limbo cell, channel `limbo:<objectId>`); or stowed (a parked ship of a dormant space bubble, channel `limbo:<objectId>`, frame kept). Its sub-channel always says which.
> 5. **Groups move atomically** only through: `AssignGroup` (spawn/respawn/delivery, velocity zeroed), `ChangeGroupFrame` (split/merge/join/roam exit, velocity kept), `EnterSupercruise`, `DropGroup` (window/anywhere/glide), `ForceDrop` (logout, emergency, later interdiction), `StowGroup`/`UnstowGroup`. Each is server-mediated and epoch-sequenced, and **flips visibility only after the group's clients acknowledge the new frame** (process-local protocol). A single group is hidden from its old observers before its mirror moves; your BubblePolicy's merges, splits and roam exits are **batches** of `ChangeGroupFrame` moves with **one visibility flip per batch**, on the last ack or at the deadline L_max (a straggler flips alone on its own ack, never before). **Group composition** (your question): a ship plus its seated characters; `Ship.prefab` has one seat, so one client per group in v1; missiles are not group members (server records), and a missile self-destructs when its target changes frame or at its bubble's roam limit; no drones or towed objects; on-foot characters are groups of one; no unseated character stays in a dynamic space bubble. **Supercruise entry and drop are the handoff seam**; a cross-process move is entry on process A, registry handoff, drop on process B. **Low cruise** (≤ 4 km/s) exists only inside body bubbles' local zones, and leaving the zone always promotes to supercruise: a frame change out, and so a handoff moment. **BubblePolicy treats a group in low cruise as in transit** (not a merge or split candidate) until it promotes or drops below 250 m/s.

> 1. **Double types for BubblePolicy.** Your spec's inputs use `Unity.Mathematics.double3`; our `Game.Universe` asmdef references only LiteNetLib and already has `UniversePosition` (places) and `Double3` (offsets, velocities). > 2. **Forced arrivals never hold (our extension).** Your arrival rule may return `Hold` + `DropIntoConflict` when a merge would force a cut and a move of up to 5 km does not help, but a logout or emergency drop, a respawn, a delivery, a login or a resume cannot wait (a login resolves synchronously). > 3. **The visible range of groups in no bubble.** A group in supercruise, stowed or in limbo has A = A0, fixed: it has no f or roam to cap by, and a supercruise |v| would query the whole grid, which holds our limbo lattice.

It is a separate optional variant as in your Part 2 §5 (our `WriteCompactState`, same rules, at most once per variant per ship per build), and your sender picks the variant per recipient; the kit loop, which cannot choose per recipient, sends mode 3 for bubble states on large maps. **Layout:** about 28 B with the 2-byte header (26 B after it, your "about 26 B"): position 3 × float32 relative to the bubble's slot origin S (no worse than our sim float budget); rotation smallest-three in 32 bits; velocity 3 × half-float; angular velocity 3 × int8 scaled to the ship's maximum turn rate; one state/teleport byte. ### B.4 SlotLayout (replaces the BubbleGrid spiral for non-collapsed maps)

`Assets/_Game/Scripts/Universe/SlotLayout.cs` is pure and deterministic. It is built as `SlotLayout.Build(hostedBodies, roamS, m)`, **over the bodies this process hosts**. In v1 that is all of the system's bodies, but the meshing work may host fewer.

- **Planet slots** are static, one per hosted landable planet.
  - The largest `R_b` goes at (0, 0, 0).
  - Up to four more go on +x, −x, +z, −z at a distance of `R_b0 + R_bi + m`.
  - Each planet's **server landscape instance** is created disabled at process start, in its slot. The height cache is shared per texture set (SgtLandscapeTextures.cs:28). The instance is enabled while its bubble is active and disabled, with its colliders released, when dormant.
  - More than 5 hosted landable planets, or any secondary planet over 31 mm, is a **hosting error** raised when the process starts. The per-system content validator (§H.6) only checks that each planet would fit at the origin (R_b ≤ 262 km).
- **Space slots** are dynamic and recycled.
  - They sit on two planes at `y = ±Y_s`, where `Y_s = R_b,max + roam_S + m` (R_b,max = 0 when no landable planet is hosted).
  - Each plane is a square lattice at pitch `P_S = 2·roam_S + m = 70 km`.
  - Allocation is best precision first: smallest Chebyshev ring, then smallest Manhattan distance, alternating planes.
  - The cap is every slot whose worst axis is ≤ 15.6 mm (98 slots in both examples below).
- **Limbo** is a lattice of **per-group cells**: 2 km pitch within ±100 km in XZ, at y = −1,000 km. That is 10,000 cells, well above the kit kill plane (DeadY is −1e9, SpaceCombatDemo.asset:32). Supercruising groups, pending arrivals and stowed ships share it.
- **Constants:** `roam_S = 30 km`, `m = 10 km`.
